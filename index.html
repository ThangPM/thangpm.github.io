From tasks to useful results

Solid: task flow. Dashed: queries, context, and evaluation observations.

Start with the task.

Define the goal and available inputs: requests, data, documents, tools, and services.

Choose useful context.

Retrieve relevant knowledge when needed; not every task requires search or document evidence.

Coordinate the work.

Give task agents relevant context, with a harness supporting model execution and tool use.

Reason and use tools.

Combine context and tool results to produce answers, summaries, analyses, or task-appropriate actions.

Keep results inspectable.

Preserve relevant source links and tool records; connect factual claims to evidence when applicable.

Inspect the whole run.

Inspect task success, output quality, tool behavior, execution traces, and responsiveness.

How I work

Follow the problem, not the boundary

Trace failures across the full system, question assumptions, and stay with the issue through diagnosis, implementation, and verification.

Let evidence challenge the idea

Use controlled comparisons to understand quality, responsiveness, and resource trade-offs. Report mixed results honestly and revise conclusions when the evidence changes.

Build for shared ownership

Build systems others can use independently, share findings early, and invite challenges. Review for correctness, not personal preference.

Research depth. Engineering experience.

I’m a Senior Machine Learning Engineer in Adobe’s Document Cloud Artificial Intelligence (DCAI) team, working on retrieval, grounded answers, evaluation, and production reliability. My experience spans software engineering, applied ML research, and production AI, connecting research questions with the practical demands of building useful systems.

I started by building communication applications as a software engineer, then moved into biomedical text mining at AIST in Japan. During my doctoral research, I completed three internships at Adobe Research, working with Trung Bui, Franck Dernoncourt, and Seunghyun Yoon before joining Adobe full-time in March 2025.

I earned my Ph.D. in Computer Science at Auburn University, advised by Prof. Anh Nguyen. My research explored how language and vision models understand their inputs, with publications at ACL, AACL, EACL, and NAACL. Our work on language understanding was featured in MIT Technology Review. I hold an Honors bachelor’s degree from HCMUS in Vietnam.
